I'm Matthew Chin — an undergraduate at Harvard studying mathematics,
statistics, and computer science, working at the intersection of
quantitative research, machine learning, and systems that are ambitious
enough to be interesting.

## Trajectory

The through-line, in retrospect: I keep choosing problems where a system's
_movement_ is the object of study. Options surfaces move against their
own quotes. Pollution moves through a city's terrain. Learned models move
— or fail to — when the data distribution shifts underneath them.
Institutions move slowest of all, which is what makes startups and
venture interesting.

That has taken concrete forms: a BTC implied-volatility surface proxy
evaluated against Deribit quotes; an advection–diffusion model of
pollution dispersion across Hong Kong; research on rotation-invariant
neural constitutive models; a market-making bot for a live trading
competition; software and product work on localization at Code.org; and
private-markets work across aerospace, defense, and enterprise
technology.

## Problems I'm drawn to

Problems where the mathematics is honest about its own limits — where
part of the work is characterizing what the data _cannot_ tell you.
Problems that cross a disciplinary boundary, because the interesting
failure modes usually live at the seam. And problems with a real system
at the end: a model someone will actually trade, breathe, or build on.

## Working principles

_Drafted by me for this site; principles I try to hold, not claims of
mastery._

- Prefer models that expose uncertainty rather than conceal it.
- Treat implementation as part of understanding — if I can't build it,
  I don't understand it yet.
- Follow difficult questions across disciplinary boundaries.
- Distinguish fitting the market from forecasting reality.
- Write to clarify what is not yet understood.
