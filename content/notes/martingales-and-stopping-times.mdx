Working notes from a probability course, kept because the optional
stopping theorem is the single cleanest bridge between measure theory and
trading intuition.

<Definition title="Martingale">
  A process $(X_n)$ adapted to a filtration $(\mathcal{F}_n)$ with
  $\mathbb{E}|X_n| < \infty$ is a martingale if
  $\mathbb{E}[X_{n+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_n] = X_n$.
</Definition>

The filtration is doing real work in that sentence: it is the formal
model of _information_, and "adapted" means you cannot peek ahead. A
martingale is a fair game relative to a specific information flow — the
same process can fail to be a martingale for a better-informed observer.

<Definition title="Stopping time">
  A random time $\tau$ is a stopping time if $\{\tau \le n\} \in
  \mathcal{F}_n$ for every $n$ — deciding to stop may use only
  information available at the moment of stopping.
</Definition>

"Sell at the top" is a random time but not a stopping time; you need the
future to recognize the top. Most trading-rule folklore dissolves into
this one measurability condition.

<Theorem title="Optional stopping">
  If $(X_n)$ is a martingale and $\tau$ a stopping time, then
  $\mathbb{E}[X_\tau] = \mathbb{E}[X_0]$ under any of: (i) $\tau$ bounded;
  (ii) $\tau$ a.s. finite and $(X_{n\wedge\tau})$ uniformly integrable;
  (iii) increments bounded and $\mathbb{E}[\tau] < \infty$.
</Theorem>

The hypotheses are not decoration. The doubling martingale — bet 1, then
2, then 4 on fair coin flips, stopping at the first win — has
$\mathbb{E}[X_\tau] = 1 \ne 0 = \mathbb{E}[X_0]$. It violates every
condition at once: $\tau$ is unbounded, the stopped process is not
uniformly integrable, increments are unbounded.

<MarginNote index="1">
  The finance reading: the doubling strategy manufactures its sure win by accepting
  unbounded intermediate losses. Every "sure thing" I have seen since decomposes into
  exactly this trade.
</MarginNote>

What the theorem says positively: with any _admissible_ exit rule — one
that respects your information and your capital constraints — a fair
game stays fair. Edge must come from the game itself being unfair in
your favor, never from the cleverness of the exit.
