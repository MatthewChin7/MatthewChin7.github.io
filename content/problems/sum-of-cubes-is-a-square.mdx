Write $T_n = 1 + 2 + \cdots + n = \dfrac{n(n+1)}{2}$. We prove
$\sum_{k=1}^{n} k^3 = T_n^2$ by induction.

**Base case.** For $n = 1$, both sides equal $1$.

**Inductive step.** Suppose $\sum_{k=1}^{n} k^3 = T_n^2$. Then

$$
\sum_{k=1}^{n+1} k^3 = T_n^2 + (n+1)^3.
$$

Since $T_{n+1} = T_n + (n+1)$,

$$
T_{n+1}^2 = T_n^2 + 2(n+1)T_n + (n+1)^2
= T_n^2 + (n+1)\big[\, 2T_n + (n+1) \,\big].
$$

Now $2T_n + (n+1) = n(n+1) + (n+1) = (n+1)^2$, so the bracket contributes
$(n+1)^3$ and we get exactly $T_{n+1}^2$. $\blacksquare$

The identity has a nice visual proof too: a square of side $T_n$ can be cut
into $L$-shaped gnomons, the $k$-th of which has area $k^3$.
