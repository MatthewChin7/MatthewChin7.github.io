Let $M = \max(X_1, X_2)$ where $X_1, X_2$ are independent and uniform on
$\{1,\dots,6\}$. The clean route is to find the distribution of $M$ first.

Since $M \le k$ exactly when _both_ dice are $\le k$,

$$
\Pr[M \le k] = \left(\frac{k}{6}\right)^2, \qquad k = 1,\dots,6.
$$

So the point mass is the difference of consecutive CDF values:

$$
\Pr[M = k] = \frac{k^2 - (k-1)^2}{36} = \frac{2k-1}{36}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\mathbb{E}[M] = \sum_{k=1}^{6} k \cdot \frac{2k-1}{36}
= \frac{1}{36}\sum_{k=1}^{6} (2k^2 - k)
= \frac{2\cdot 91 - 21}{36}
= \frac{161}{36} \approx 4.47.
$$

A useful sanity check: the expectation of the _minimum_ comes out to
$\tfrac{91}{36} \approx 2.53$, and $\mathbb{E}[\max] + \mathbb{E}[\min] = 7 =
\mathbb{E}[X_1] + \mathbb{E}[X_2]$, as it must.
